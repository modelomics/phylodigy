# Homology and Convergence

Similar graph structure can reflect shared ancestry, copied components,
independent construction, common framework lowering, or common constraints.
Graph similarity alone does not choose among them.

## Structural evidence

Automatically discovered characters and audited graph edits localize what is
shared or changed without presupposing a technique name. Exact code history,
release provenance, dates, and paper annotations provide independent evidence
for historical interpretation.

A primitive alignment is a directional comparison, not a reconstruction of
history. Its add/remove replay operations reverse when the endpoints reverse.
Likewise, a deterministic match selected in a solver-underidentified local
orbit is not evidence that those individual occurrences are homologous.
Historical gain, loss, and homology require an inferred tree or network plus
independent evidence and an explicit evolutionary model.

A frequent graph signature has weaker specificity than a rare, complex aligned
region, but recurrence is estimated from the frozen corpus or a fitted model.
It is not assigned by a catalog.

## Tree and network views

A tree-like vertical backbone is useful when event-weighted distances are
approximately additive. Quartet residuals, conflicting region histories, and
component-level provenance can support lateral contact or split-network signal.

Keep these views linked to supporting graph objects. A lateral edge should
identify the aligned region or edit that motivated it whenever possible.

## Convergence controls

Estimate how often structures arise independently across repositories,
frameworks, tasks, and hardware constraints. Include separately implemented
models and framework-default patterns as negative controls.

Paper vocabulary must be blinded during structural discovery. Otherwise a
name-based grouping can make convergence look like inheritance by construction.

## Limits of the analogy

Software can be copied exactly, rewritten deliberately, merged from many
sources, or generated. One developer can know several systems simultaneously.
The intended result is therefore a phylogenetic hypothesis with explicit
reticulate signal, not a claim that software follows biological mechanisms.
